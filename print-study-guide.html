1. Read the Passage

Read the passage at least twice. Read the surrounding verses or chapter, and note who is speaking, who is being addressed, and what is happening.

What stands out? Are there repeated words, commands, promises, contrasts, or questions?

2. Observe — What Does It Say?

Record details that are actually in the text before deciding what they mean.

  • Who is involved, and what is the setting?
  • What happens before and after this passage?
  • What words or ideas are repeated or emphasized?

3. Interpret — What Did It Mean?

Consider the book's purpose, original audience, historical setting, and literary form. Avoid making a verse say more—or less—than its context supports.

What is the author's main point? Which details in the passage support your conclusion?

4. Compare Scripture with Scripture

Write down related passages. Read each one in its own context, and note both the connections and the differences.

Cross-references:

How do these passages help clarify the topic?

5. Apply — How Should I Respond?

Consider what the passage teaches about God, faith, character, relationships, worship, or daily choices. Application should follow careful understanding.

Is there a truth to believe, a promise to trust, a command to consider, or an action to take?

6. Questions to Explore

List anything that remains unclear. Check other relevant passages and reliable study resources before settling on an answer.
