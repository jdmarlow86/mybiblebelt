Salvation Through Christ

Read: John 3:16-17; Romans 3:23-24; Ephesians 2:8-10; Romans 10:9-13.

Scripture teaches that all people need God's grace and that salvation is a gift received through faith in Jesus Christ, not something earned by good works. Good works are the fruit of a changed life, not a way to purchase God's favor.

Reflect: What do these passages say about God's initiative, our response, and the place of good works in a Christian life?

Baptism and New Life

Read: Matthew 28:18-20; Acts 2:37-41; Romans 6:1-4; Colossians 2:12.

Jesus instructed His followers to make disciples and baptize them. The New Testament connects baptism with repentance, faith, identification with Christ, and the beginning of a new way of life. Christian traditions differ on some details of baptism, so compare each passage in context.

Reflect: How do these passages connect belief, repentance, baptism, and discipleship?

The Sanctuary Service

Read: Exodus 25:1-9; Leviticus 16:15-22; Hebrews 8:1-6; Hebrews 9:11-14, 23-28.

The tabernacle and its services were part of Israel's worship and taught lessons about holiness, sacrifice, cleansing, and access to God. Hebrews connects the earlier sanctuary imagery with Jesus' priestly ministry and His once-for-all sacrifice.

Reflect: What similarities and differences do you notice between the earthly sanctuary described in the Old Testament and the work of Christ described in Hebrews?

God's Law and Grace

Read: Exodus 20:1-17; Matthew 5:17-20; Matthew 22:34-40; Romans 3:31; Romans 6:14; James 2:8-12.

The Bible presents God's commands alongside His mercy and grace. Jesus summarized the law's central duties as love for God and love for neighbor. Christians interpret the relationship between the old covenant law and life in Christ in different ways; reading passages together helps avoid taking any one verse out of context.

Reflect: How does love for God and neighbor shape the way Jesus' followers understand obedience?

Reading the Gospels

Read: Mark 1:1-15; Luke 4:16-21; John 1:1-18; Matthew 5-7.

Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John each present the life, teaching, death, and resurrection of Jesus. Read a passage carefully, note its setting and audience, and compare parallel accounts when appropriate. Pay attention to what the passage reveals about Jesus and the kingdom of God.

Reflect: What does your chosen passage show Jesus doing or teaching, and how do the people around Him respond?

Paul's Letters

Read: Romans 5:1-11; 1 Corinthians 13; Galatians 5:13-26; Ephesians 4:1-16.

Paul's letters address real questions and challenges in early Christian communities. To understand a letter, identify its audience, the issue being discussed, and how each paragraph fits into the larger argument. Avoid building a conclusion from a sentence separated from its context.

Reflect: What problem or question is Paul addressing, and what response does he encourage?

Wisdom Literature

Read: Psalm 1; Psalm 23; Psalm 51; Proverbs 3:1-12; Proverbs 8:1-11.

Psalms express prayer, praise, grief, confession, and trust. Proverbs offers concise instruction about wise and foolish choices. Notice the literary form: poetry often uses imagery and parallel lines, while proverbs commonly express general wisdom rather than a guarantee that every situation will unfold identically.

Reflect: What does the passage teach about wisdom, worship, relationships, or trusting God?

The Prophets

Read: Isaiah 1:10-20; Isaiah 53; Jeremiah 31:31-34; Micah 6:6-8.

The biblical prophets spoke to particular people and circumstances, often calling for justice, faithfulness, repentance, and hope. Consider the original historical setting and distinguish the prophet's direct message to that audience from later applications readers may draw.

Reflect: What injustice or unfaithfulness is addressed, and what does the passage reveal about God's character?

Inductive Bible Study Method

Try three steps: Observation—what does the passage actually say? Interpretation—what did it mean in its original context? Application—how should its message shape belief or action today?

Read the whole paragraph or chapter, note repeated words and contrasts, ask who is speaking and to whom, and compare related passages before drawing a conclusion.

Understanding Context

Context includes the verses around a passage, the purpose of the book, its original audience, historical setting, and literary form. A verse should not be made to say something that conflicts with the author's point in the surrounding passage.

Practice: Read Matthew 4:5-7 and notice how Jesus responds when Scripture is quoted without being used faithfully.

Cross-Reference Guide

Cross-references connect passages that share a theme, quotation, image, or idea. For example, compare the creation account in Genesis 2:1-3 with the Sabbath command in Exodus 20:8-11, and then read how Jesus speaks about the Sabbath in Mark 2:27-28.

Read each reference in its own context; a shared word does not automatically mean two passages make exactly the same point.

Using a Concordance

A concordance helps locate verses where a word appears. Search for key terms, read several results in context, and check whether a translation uses the same English word for different original-language words.

Practice: Look up the word “grace” and compare Ephesians 2:8-10 with Titus 2:11-14. What does each passage say grace does?

The Sabbath - God's Holy Day

"Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: But the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God: in it thou shalt not do any work..." - Exodus 20:8-10

The Sabbath is the seventh day of the week - Saturday. It was established at Creation when God rested on the seventh day and sanctified it (Genesis 2:1-3). Jesus declared Himself "Lord of the Sabbath" (Matthew 12:8) and kept the Sabbath throughout His earthly ministry.

The Sabbath is:

  • A memorial of Creation
  • A sign of sanctification
  • A day of delight and spiritual refreshment
  • A symbol of our rest in Christ

"If thou turn away thy foot from the sabbath, from doing thy pleasure on my holy day; and call the sabbath a delight, the holy of the LORD, honourable; and shalt honour him, not doing thine own ways, nor finding thine own pleasure, nor speaking thine own words: Then shalt thou delight thyself in the LORD..." - Isaiah 58:13-14

The Second Coming of Christ

"For the Lord himself shall descend from heaven with a shout, with the voice of the archangel, and with the trump of God: and the dead in Christ shall rise first: Then we which are alive and remain shall be caught up together with them in the clouds, to meet the Lord in the air: and so shall we ever be with the Lord." - 1 Thessalonians 4:16-17

The Second Coming of Jesus Christ is the blessed hope of the Christian faith. Every eye shall see Him, and He will come with power and great glory to redeem His people and establish His everlasting kingdom.

Signs of His coming include:

  • Wars and rumors of wars
  • Earthquakes, famines, and pestilences
  • Moral decay and lawlessness
  • The gospel preached to all nations
